The receipt

Thermal paper fades in about a year. Most warranties last two. The proof dies before the cover does.

The date

Nobody remembers that the washing machine was bought in March. The manufacturer will ask.

The claim

By the time something breaks, you have three days left and no serial number to hand.

Adding things

Six ways in. All of them end in the same form.

Point the camera at whatever you have — the box, the receipt, the thing itself — and the details come out filled in. Nothing is saved until you tap Save.

Camera

Scan the product

Barcode, QR or serial. The code becomes a brand, a model number and a typical warranty length.

Camera

Scan the receipt

Edge-detected capture, then the shop, the date, the price and the item are read off it. On a long receipt it picks the expensive thing you'd actually claim on.

Camera

Photograph it

Take a picture of the product. The background is cut away and what's left becomes its photo in your list.

Library

Choose a photo

Already took a picture of the receipt months ago? Pick it, and the app spots that it's a receipt and reads it.

Files

Import a document

A warranty card, an emailed invoice, a PDF. Or paste the link to the product page you bought from.

Safari

Share from the shop

Share any product page to Product Guard the moment you order. It reads the page and starts the record for you.

Where your data goes

Your receipts never leave the phone.

Reading a receipt, filling the form, sorting things into categories, writing a claim — all of it runs on your device using Apple's on-device models. There is no account to create and no database of users anywhere.

Stays on your phone

Always. No setting to get wrong.

  • Receipt images and product photos
  • What you paid, where, and when
  • Serial numbers
  • Claim drafts and the faults you describe
  • Search across your own products

Leaves only if you ask

Off by default · One switch in Settings

  • A brand and model you're looking up
  • The question you type into the assistant
  • A product page you chose to share
  • Your records to your own private iCloud, if you're signed in

The assistant is switched off until you turn it on, and its consent screen names every field that leaves. Read the privacy policy.

When something breaks

The bit everyone gets wrong is the letter.

You have the cover, you have the dates, and then you sit staring at a support form. Product Guard writes the first draft using what it already knows about the purchase.

Told before, not after

Reminders arrive on your schedule, at a time you choose. They're scheduled by your phone — no server, no push, works with the plane on.

30 days 14 days 7 days 1 day On the day 09:00

Change a date and every pending reminder is rebuilt, so what the screen shows is what your phone will actually deliver.

A claim, already written

Describe the fault in a sentence. Out comes a claim message with the model, the serial, the retailer and the purchase date already in it. Edit it, copy it, send it yourself — the app never sends anything for you.

Dear Sony Support, I am writing regarding my WH-1000XM5 headphones (serial S5X-8841-QP), purchased from Currys on 12 August 2024 and covered until 12 August 2026. The right earcup has stopped producing sound
